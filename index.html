Research Program

The research programme is organized around four complementary areas investigating how mathematical structure, information, geometry, and physical organization may be related.

01 — Structural Information Evolution

Study of information, entropy, and structural organization across scales, with an emphasis on how macroscopic organization may emerge from underlying informational dynamics.

02 — Emergent Geometry & Causality

Mathematical approaches to causal structure, Lorentzian geometry, light-cone algebra, quotient constructions, and pre-geometric descriptions of spacetime.

03 — Spectral & Geometric Dynamics

Investigation of spectral dimension, scale-dependent geometry, irreversible flow, fractal structures, and related geometric dynamics.

04 — Mathematical Foundations & Computation

Research in representation problems, additive combinatorics, gap geometry, computational enumeration, and reproducible computational analysis of mathematical conjectures.

Current Research — August 2026

Sun's (2,4,6,8) Binomial Representation Conjecture V23.4 Completed

The V23.4 phase completed an exhaustive computational audit of the candidate n = 896,315,812,331,399 for the representation $$ n = \binom{w}{2} + \binom{x}{4} + \binom{y}{6} + \binom{z}{8}. $$ Explicit finite parameter bounds were established and the resulting search domain was evaluated using exact integer arithmetic.

The computation examined 2,818,953,028 admissible (z,y,x) triples and found no representation of the candidate within the certified search domain.

The result is currently classified as a computational counterexample candidate, pending independent replication and further mathematical audit. The original proof programme has therefore been concluded at V23.4 rather than continued under the original conjecture.

The V23.4 investigation also produced additional modular, Cantor-Pascal, and scale-structure diagnostics. These results are retained as mathematical observations and possible starting points for subsequent research, but are not presented as proofs of non-representability.

Research Statement

The research approach emphasizes mathematical structure, explicit definitions, testable consequences, and reproducible investigation across mathematics and theoretical physics.

Different levels of evidence are kept conceptually separate: established mathematical results, computational observations, theoretical constructions, and exploratory hypotheses are not treated as equivalent forms of evidence.

Computational work is used both as a discovery mechanism and as an independent audit of mathematical claims. When new evidence contradicts a conjecture or exposes a limitation in a proof programme, the appropriate response is to verify the result, revise the claim, and reassess the underlying mathematical structure.

Research and Versioning Policy

Research outputs are archived through persistent scholarly identifiers including DOI and ORCID. Research versions may evolve as mathematical and computational results are independently examined. The website distinguishes between established mathematical results, computational audits, candidate counterexamples, theoretical frameworks, and exploratory research. A computational candidate is not described as a confirmed mathematical counterexample until the underlying computation has been independently reproduced and the relevant mathematical assumptions and search bounds have been fully audited.
