Current Research Status — September 2026

Sun's (2,4,6,8) Binomial Representation Conjecture V23.4 Completed

The V23.4 phase completed an exhaustive computational investigation of the candidate \(n^* = 896,315,812,331,399\) for the representation \[ n = \binom{w}{2} + \binom{x}{4} + \binom{y}{6} + \binom{z}{8}. \]

Explicit finite parameter bounds were established, and the resulting search domain was evaluated using exact integer arithmetic. The computation examined 2,818,953,028 admissible \((z,y,x)\) triples and found no representation of the candidate within the certified search domain.

The result is currently classified as a computational counterexample candidate , rather than a confirmed mathematical counterexample. Independent replication and further mathematical audit remain necessary before a stronger conclusion can be made.

The original proof programme has therefore been concluded at V23.4 and is not being continued under the original conjectural assumption.

The V23.4 investigation also established the modular result \[ \mathcal{D}_3 \pmod{231} = \mathbb{Z}/231\mathbb{Z}, \] which rules out a local residue obstruction at that modulus. Additional Cantor–Pascal and scale-structure observations are retained as exploratory mathematical diagnostics.

Research Methodology

The programme places particular emphasis on distinguishing what has been mathematically proved, computationally verified, theoretically constructed, and provisionally hypothesized.

01 — Explicit Definitions

Mathematical objects, assumptions, parameter domains, and logical dependencies are stated explicitly whenever possible.

02 — Exact Computation

Computational experiments use exact arithmetic and finite search bounds where the mathematical problem permits.

03 — Independent Verification

Important computational claims are treated as provisional until independently reproduced and audited.

04 — Evidence Separation

Proofs, computational observations, theoretical constructions, and exploratory hypotheses are kept conceptually distinct.

Research Statement

The research programme explores whether information-theoretic and structural principles can provide mathematically coherent descriptions of how geometry, causality, complexity, and physical organization emerge across scales.

The work combines mathematical analysis, information-theoretic ideas, complex-systems perspectives, geometric structures, theoretical modelling, and computational methods.

A central methodological principle is to maintain a clear distinction between established mathematical results, verified computational observations, theoretical constructions, and open hypotheses.

When computational or mathematical evidence challenges an existing conjecture, the research process treats verification, revision, and reassessment as integral parts of mathematical investigation.
